## Racking with WBS power supplies?

_WBS RACKMOUNTING · Oct 22, 2011|44_

> Well the M460E is a mic only module with EQ. Any regulated WBS supply will work. Ideally you would use one M625 and one M648 - but they are huge and way over kill for a few modules. You can locate lots of physically smaller linear power-supplies like JLM or try Tristian's. Keep the big supplies for your self and your new board.

## M441 noise at -40dB pos

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Mar 17, 2012|45_

> Hello everyone,
>
>  I have a pair of M441M that I'm in the process of racking. One was in working condition, and the other I had to reassemble because all the wiring from the PCBs to the front panel had been dismantled. Both are stock, except for two capacitors connected to the reverb send pot on the second one I had to change, because one somehow got mangled.
>
>  I had run tests and they behave pretty well (save the awfully scratchy switches), except for one quite annoying quirk: I find the noise is very high on the -40dB position of the gain switch. In regard to the -50dB position, the signal goes down 10dB as expected, but the noise jumps up two-three folds!
>
>  I'like to know if other users of this module are familiar/annoyed/found a cure for this condition. To this point the tests are pretty superficial: I had run them speaking to an ordinary dynamis mic (AKG D125). I haved hacked a 30 Volt shitching power supply to give me the needed 48V. 
>
>  Clues I got when reading the schematic tell me there might be some problem with the design of the gain structure of the front end: at the -40dB position two 580 Ohm resistors are inserted in the signal path between the mic and the transformer, obviously to attenuate the signal. But at first view that does not seem to be a proper pad circuit. And the gain remains the same as the -60dB position! Maybe this structure should be redesigned... Anyone want to take a look?
>
>  This is all part of a vast program to get me extrenal preamps: I got also a pair of M460E modules to do, and will make a 4-pack of Chilton CM-2 channel strips. Stay tuned!
>
>  Pierre Olivier

## 460a distortion

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Jan 28, 2013|46_

> NO FUN AT ALL!!!! For you are having loads of it - right!?!?!
>
> OK the modules in presentation look very nice - they look like M460E modules.
>
> So still a bit of an issue - lets make a chart/map. Do a sketch (LARGE ONE) of the regulator all transistors, resistors, diodes, etc.. Give your self room around each device to neatly write in voltages. Take a nice and happy M460 and your volt meter, and measure everything with a consistent ground (chassis somewhere but relatively the same point each time please). Go through the two TIPS, the transistors, each side of the diodes, etc... and write this on your chart/map thing. Next compare the numbers of the good module vs. the sick one (again no daughter boards in place, etc..). this will tell you who is ill - as you compare the good measurements against the sick modules measurements, you will see that going to the emitter to T4, the voltages are off significantly, and the re resistor that feeds this emitter of T4 has silly voltages on either side of it. Thus a resistor is dead - replace that sucker and test again. Work from left to right or top to bottom, or what ever. Just give yourself some consistency in the methodology. We should drop the supply voltage a smidge as these modules are quite old, and you really do not need all that voltage or hose-power sitting there. As an operator you would never experience anything different if the module was short a few volts (not necessarily starving it, just dimming the lights a bit) you would just be extending the life of some of the old components.
>
> Are your TIP's on a black heat sink or bolted to the modules frame? If I scroll down I see a heat sink - but are the A's like that as well?? For the hidden resistor the R33 might be poo so please don't forget to measure it!!

## 1982 WBS Frame restoration and customization: Project started October 2011

_MY WBS CONSOLE · Nov 12, 2013|46_

> 1982 WBS Frame restoration and customization
>
> At this point.. after removing the D.A. system.. i hadn't put any power in the console. 
>
> Still too much stuff to get used to before risking having smoke in there.. 
>
> (It seems that i really knew the whats/wheres/whys and whens but I had really good advice from very generous people here on the WBSPS)
>
> So.. Power Supplies... the back view of where the PSU (M625B) slide from the front. 2 power supplies. Giving +/- 25v D.C. needed to operate the audio signal of each input channels. 
>
> When I bought the console.. I only got the frame with its accessories.. but no input channels.. the sellers wanted to keep their modules (M460LA) and rack them. I only had 2x M460E that i bought a year ago in the project of racking them too. But since I just got a frame.. I would eventually be able to test lots of the things in the console with them. 
>
> So... Let the "beeping" start!! O.o
>
> MANY hours on beeping and figuring what is where regarding the pinout of each section. 
>
> As the Buss selector modules (M468K) were sitting on the top below the VU panels. I arguably knew that.. (at this point the PDF section of the WBSPS became my "day-to-day-go-to" IMMENSE THANK YOU to Anthony P. Kuzub for building and accumulating all these manuals and knitting them so we could all benefit from them. VERY long and exhausting work there. Again.. Thank you Tone.)... so... I knew that these modules were to route the signal from each input modules to the master section. In my case S1 to S6 and M1-M2. (S=Subs/M=Main/Master) I knew that below this section.. it had to be the input channels by how the modules (M460E) were designed physically and by how the slots appeared in the bucket inside the console. 
>
> After figuring what from the Input modules (M460E) was sitting on the patch bays, like; Mic input, Line input, inserts. Direct outs were not feeded to the patch bays. I eventually had to wire them. So after that.. the longest "beeping" passion went into the master section.. where all ancillaries were sitting. But since the frame didn't come with any master modules, Aux modules, Monitor modules, compressors or anything.. that was a bit mind boggling.

— **pawajari**, Nov 15, 2013|46
